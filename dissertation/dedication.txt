I thank my family and friends for supporting me throughout this journey. Pursuing higher education as a first-generation citizen and student is hard. Even though I have dedicated these years to learning and practicing how to support students like me, that wasn't enough. I desperately needed connection to the people and cultures that keep me grounded in who I am and why I am here. Time and time again when I felt isolated, weak, and guilty. In constant mourning of the connections I have not been able to maintain and nurture, because I felt like I had to invest my energy in making sure I was enough in the eyes of these institutions that I trusted to be a vehicle for supporting my people. As I delved deeper and deeper into this space, this trust dwindled and along with my mental stability. But my family and friends were always there for me unconditionally. We didn't know what I needed or what the answers were because this was a new experience, and very few people have personally experienced what it is like to be at these intersections. They were there to remind me that I chose to stand at these intersections for a reason. And they were there to remind that no matter what the outcome, I am enough and what I have done with my life is enough.

I also dedicate this to future students who find themselves navigating a similar experience. 
If you have felt unsure of whether you belong or were enough, I hope this inspires you to question what standards you are holding yourself up to and why. Who do they serve? Who will you serve? Whatever the answer, I hope you are able to stay grounded in the values and visions shared by the people who are your home.

I would also like to thank my mentors, collaborators, and peers.
